Planning draft. Check that the suggested example and assessment match this lesson title before teaching.

Learning and success criteria

  • Explain and apply the key idea in L1: Images: Pixels and resolution.
  • Explain the effect on the represented data or storage and relate it to the intended use.

Subject knowledge and resources

  • Compare two encodings of a tiny image, text sample or audio clip. Identify what each stored value represents and calculate the relevant storage difference.
  • Anticipated misconception: Increasing fidelity can raise storage requirements even when the subject of the file stays the same.
  • Prepare the worked example, a parallel pupil task and a model answer.

45-minute sequence

TimeTeaching and learning
0–5 minRetrieve one idea from Summative Assessment and connect it to today’s lesson.
5–15 minCompare two encodings of a tiny image, text sample or audio clip. Identify what each stored value represents and calculate the relevant storage difference.
15–23 minWhat would change if you increased one relevant resolution or precision setting? Expected evidence: Explain the effect on the represented data or storage and relate it to the intended use. If pupils miss the key idea, re-model and ask a parallel question.
23–40 minChoose a representation for a stated purpose. Annotate the data or calculate storage, then explain the quality trade-off.
40–45 minAsk pupils to answer a fresh version of the check. Review their reasoning before planning the next lesson.

Adaptive teaching and challenge

  • Offer a partially worked model and a vocabulary reference where useful. Agree any individual adjustments before teaching.
  • Ask pupils to predict what changes when one input, rule or design choice is varied.
