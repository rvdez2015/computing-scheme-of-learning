Planning draft. Adapt resources and support for your class.

Learning and success criteria

  • Explain and apply the key idea in The fetch-decode-execute cycle, including its effect on registers..
  • Name the CIR and PC correctly and show a coherent register trace.

Subject knowledge and resources

  • Trace one simplified instruction with PC = 20. Show MAR = 20, the fetched instruction through MDR into CIR, and the PC moving to the next instruction before decode and execute.
  • Anticipated misconception: The PC holds an address and the CIR holds the instruction currently being decoded or executed.
  • Prepare the worked example, a parallel pupil task and a model answer.

45-minute sequence

TimeTeaching and learning
0–5 minRetrieve one idea from The Arithmetic and Logic Unit; ALU, Control Unit and Registers (Program Counter; PC, Accumulator; ACC, Memory Address Register; MAR, Memory Data Register; MDR, Current Instruction Register; CIR). Buses: data, address and control: how this relates to assembly language programs. and connect it to today’s lesson.
5–15 minTrace one simplified instruction with PC = 20. Show MAR = 20, the fetched instruction through MDR into CIR, and the PC moving to the next instruction before decode and execute.
15–23 minWhich register contains the fetched instruction during decode, and which contains the next address? Expected evidence: Name the CIR and PC correctly and show a coherent register trace. If pupils miss the key idea, re-model and ask a parallel question.
23–40 minComplete a second register trace starting at address 36 and annotate the role of each register.
40–45 minAsk pupils to answer a fresh version of the check. Review their reasoning before planning the next lesson.

Adaptive teaching and challenge

  • Offer a partially worked model and a vocabulary reference where useful. Agree any individual adjustments before teaching.
  • Ask pupils to justify an alternative or test a boundary case using precise technical language.
